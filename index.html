Who Verifies the Agents?

Toward Reliable Agent Development

Verification is the bottleneck between fragile prototypes and scalable, reliable agent systems. This workshop convenes researchers and practitioners to make verification a first-class discipline in agent development.

Sydney, Australia · Dec 11 or 12, 2026 · Co-located with NeurIPS

About the Workshop

Agent development is undergoing a crucial shift. Despite remarkable advances in agents that reason, plan, and operate in open-ended environments, their construction remains fragmented, dominated by manual prompt engineering and ad-hoc scaffolding. The next leap is toward scalable, autonomous agent development grounded in systematic engineering principles that ensure reliability in dynamic environments.

The bottleneck is not a deficit of agentic frameworks but verification: the ability to reliably determine whether a change to an agent — a prompt update, a new tool integration, a change in reasoning logic — is a genuine improvement. Verification works in controlled settings (formal math, competitive programming, coding) but remains shallow and noisy in general agentic tasks, causing performance to plateau or silently regress during development. Prevailing pipelines lean on limited signals — isolated outcome rewards or LLM-as-judge scores — that are too narrow to capture long-horizon behavior and susceptible to reward hacking.

Unlocking reliable agents in practice requires verification frameworks that are broader in scope (diverse tasks and edge cases), deeper in analysis (process-level reasoning), and more heterogeneous (beyond scalar rewards). This workshop convenes researchers from reinforcement learning, agentic AI, evaluation and benchmarking, AI safety and red-teaming, simulation, and ML systems around three pillars.

Three Pillars of Verification

We invite work spanning — but not limited to — these three interconnected themes.

01

Safety & Robustness of Verification

Verification must evolve with agents: robust verifiers that prevent reward hacking, resist harness specification gaming, and maintain alignment — scaling from outcome checks to process-level signals like traces, plans, and tool-call sequences.

02

Environment-Grounded Verification & Simulators

For open-ended tasks lacking closed-form metrics, the environment itself is the ground truth. Faithful simulators of agentic tasks — from multi-agent kernel optimization to self-optimizing production systems — can become a verification infrastructure layer.

03

Diverse & Heterogeneous Verifiable Signals

Beyond scalar rewards, holistic evaluation integrates user experience, cost/latency profiles, calibration, and multimodality. A key challenge is composing these heterogeneous signals into reliable verification metrics.

Call for Papers

Overview

Despite remarkable advances in agents that reason, plan, and operate in open-ended environments, reliably verifying their improvements remains an unsolved challenge. Verification is the ability to determine whether a change to an agent — such as a prompt update, a new tool integration, or a modification to its reasoning process — produces a genuine improvement in capability or reliability.

Verification works in controlled settings (formal math, competitive programming, coding) but remains shallow and noisy for general agentic tasks, causing performance to plateau or silently regress during development. This workshop convenes researchers to tackle verification as a first-class research problem for reliable agent development.

Topics of Interest

We invite submissions across three core pillars, as well as topics at their intersection:

Pillar 1: Safety and Robustness of Verification

  • Robust verifiers that prevent reward hacking and specification gaming
  • Adversarial robustness of verifiers and red-teaming of evaluation harnesses
  • Alignment-aware verification: ensuring verifiers remain faithful as agents evolve

Pillar 2: Environment-Grounded Verification and Simulators

  • Faithful simulators as verification infrastructure for open-ended tasks
  • Multi-agent and self-optimizing systems for environment-grounded evaluation
  • Measuring agents in production: observability, monitoring, and runtime verification
  • Evolutionary and search-based methods for environment-driven agent optimization
  • Benchmarks and environment design that stress-test verification methods

Pillar 3: Diverse and Heterogeneous Verifiable Signals

  • Composing heterogeneous signals (user experience, cost/latency, calibration, multimodality) into reliable verification metrics
  • Beyond scalar rewards: holistic evaluation of agentic behavior
  • Human-in-the-loop verification and human–AI collaborative evaluation
  • Reflective and self-improving verification (agents that verify other agents)
  • Automated agent design, prompt optimization, and scaffold search with verifiable feedback
  • Formal verification of agent-generated artifacts, including the use of proof assistants and verification languages (e.g., Dafny, Rocq, and Lean)

Cross-Cutting Topics

  • Verification for meta-agents and Agent4Agent systems (agents that design, optimize, or evaluate other agents)
  • Self-evolving agents: stable improvement without collapse or reward hacking
  • Evaluation of agent-generated designs vs. human-engineered systems
  • Scalable oversight and verification for long-horizon, multi-step agent behavior
  • Cognitive and neuroscience-inspired verification frameworks

Submission Guidelines

  • Format: Papers should be between 4 to 9 pages (excluding references and appendices), using the NeurIPS 2026 template. We also welcome demo papers, which should be no more than 4 pages.
  • Dual submission policy: We welcome work that is under review or has been recently published at other venues.
  • Review: Reviews will be double blind. Reviewers will not use LLMs to evaluate submissions.
  • Presentation: Accepted papers will be presented as posters; select papers will be chosen for oral presentations or lightning talks.
  • Non-archival: The workshop is non-archival; papers will be made available on the workshop website but do not constitute formal proceedings.

Submissions and reviewing are handled through OpenReview.

Organizers will not submit work to the workshop.
