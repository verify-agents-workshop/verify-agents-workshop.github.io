Who Verifies the Agents?

Toward Reliable Agent Development

Verification is the bottleneck between fragile prototypes and scalable, reliable agent systems. This workshop convenes researchers and practitioners to make verification a first-class discipline in agent development.

San Diego, USA · December 2026 · Co-located with NeurIPS

About the Workshop

Recent advances in autonomous agents have demonstrated potential for complex reasoning and open-ended tasks. However, current agent development is often fragmented and dependent on ad-hoc methods, leading to reliability challenges where performance plateaus or regresses during iteration.

The fundamental bottleneck preventing the transition to scalable, reliable agent systems is verification: the inability to systematically determine whether a change to an agent constitutes a genuine improvement. This workshop seeks to formalize verification as a core discipline in agent development.

We convene researchers and practitioners to explore three critical pillars: developing robust verifiers that resist reward hacking, leveraging environment-grounded simulation as the ground truth for evaluation, and integrating heterogeneous signals like latency, cost, and calibration into the verification loop. By centering verification in the development lifecycle, this workshop aims to establish the foundation necessary to move toward truly autonomous and reliable agentic systems.

Three Pillars of Verification

We invite work spanning — but not limited to — these three interconnected themes.

01

Robust Verifiers

Designing verifiers and reward models that resist reward hacking, remain faithful under distribution shift, and provide trustworthy signal for whether an agent has genuinely improved.

02

Environment-Grounded Simulation

Treating interactive, environment-grounded simulation as the ground truth for evaluation — building faithful sandboxes, benchmarks, and world models that reflect real deployment conditions.

03

Heterogeneous Signals

Integrating signals beyond task success — latency, cost, calibration, robustness, and safety — into a unified verification loop that reflects the true objectives of deployed agents.

Call for Papers

Details forthcoming

We welcome submissions of original research, position papers, and works-in-progress that advance the science and practice of verifying agents. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Reward hacking, specification gaming, and robustness of verifiers
  • Environment-grounded simulation, sandboxes, and benchmarks for agent evaluation
  • Process- vs. outcome-based verification and reward modeling
  • Calibration, uncertainty, and confidence estimation in agent evaluation
  • Multi-objective evaluation: latency, cost, safety, and calibration signals
  • Human-in-the-loop and LLM-as-judge verification, and their failure modes
  • Detecting regressions and attributing improvement during iterative development
  • Reproducibility, tooling, and infrastructure for reliable agent development

Submission instructions, format, and page limits will be announced here. Check back for the submission portal link and detailed guidelines.
