Who Verifies the Agents?

Toward Reliable Agent Development

Verification is the bottleneck between fragile prototypes and scalable, reliable agent systems. This workshop convenes researchers and practitioners to make verification a first-class discipline in agent development.

Sydney, Australia · Dec 11 or 12, 2026 · Co-located with NeurIPS

About the Workshop

Agent development is undergoing a crucial shift. Despite remarkable advances in agents that reason, plan, and operate in open-ended environments, their construction remains fragmented, dominated by manual prompt engineering and ad-hoc scaffolding. The next leap is toward scalable, autonomous agent development grounded in systematic engineering principles that ensure reliability in dynamic environments.

The bottleneck is not a deficit of agentic frameworks but verification: the ability to reliably determine whether a change to an agent — a prompt update, a new tool integration, a change in reasoning logic — is a genuine improvement. Verification works in controlled settings (formal math, competitive programming, coding) but remains shallow and noisy in general agentic tasks, causing performance to plateau or silently regress during development. Prevailing pipelines lean on limited signals — isolated outcome rewards or LLM-as-judge scores — that are too narrow to capture long-horizon behavior and susceptible to reward hacking.

Unlocking reliable agents in practice requires verification frameworks that are broader in scope (diverse tasks and edge cases), deeper in analysis (process-level reasoning), and more heterogeneous (beyond scalar rewards). This workshop convenes researchers from reinforcement learning, agentic AI, evaluation and benchmarking, AI safety and red-teaming, simulation, and ML systems around three pillars.

Three Pillars of Verification

We invite work spanning — but not limited to — these three interconnected themes.

01

Safety & Robustness of Verification

Verification must evolve with agents: robust verifiers that prevent reward hacking, resist harness specification gaming, and maintain alignment — scaling from outcome checks to process-level signals like traces, plans, and tool-call sequences.

02

Environment-Grounded Verification & Simulators

For open-ended tasks lacking closed-form metrics, the environment itself is the ground truth. Faithful simulators of agentic tasks — from multi-agent kernel optimization to self-optimizing production systems — can become a verification infrastructure layer.

03

Diverse & Heterogeneous Verifiable Signals

Beyond scalar rewards, holistic evaluation integrates user experience, cost/latency profiles, calibration, and multimodality. A key challenge is composing these heterogeneous signals into reliable verification metrics.

Call for Papers

Details forthcoming

We welcome submissions of original research, position papers, and works-in-progress that advance the science and practice of verifying agents. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Reward hacking, specification gaming, and robustness of verifiers
  • Environment-grounded simulation, sandboxes, and benchmarks for agent evaluation
  • Process- vs. outcome-based verification and reward modeling
  • Calibration, uncertainty, and confidence estimation in agent evaluation
  • Multi-objective evaluation: latency, cost, safety, and calibration signals
  • Human-in-the-loop and LLM-as-judge verification, and their failure modes
  • Detecting regressions and attributing improvement during iterative development
  • Reproducibility, tooling, and infrastructure for reliable agent development

Submissions and reviewing will be handled through OpenReview, and the program will be posted on this website ahead of the conference. Organizers will not submit work to the workshop, and reviewers will not use LLMs to evaluate submissions.

The full call for papers — submission link, format, and page limits — will be announced here.
