The Bottleneck

Verification Declared the Missing Discipline of Agent Development

Despite remarkable advances in agents that reason, plan, and operate in open-ended environments, their construction remains fragmented — and the field's pipelines cannot say whether a change is a genuine improvement.

Agent development is undergoing a crucial shift. The bottleneck, researchers argue, is not a deficit of agentic frameworks but verification: the ability to reliably determine whether a change to an agent — a prompt update, a new tool integration, a change in reasoning logic — is a genuine improvement.

Verification works in controlled settings such as formal mathematics, competitive programming, and coding, but remains shallow and noisy in general agentic tasks, causing performance to plateau or silently regress during development. Prevailing pipelines lean on limited signals — isolated outcome rewards or LLM-as-judge scores — too narrow to capture long-horizon behavior and susceptible to reward hacking.

Unlocking reliable agents in practice, the organizers contend, requires verification frameworks broader in scope, deeper in analysis, and more heterogeneous than today's. The workshop convenes researchers from reinforcement learning, agentic AI, evaluation and benchmarking, AI safety, simulation, and ML systems.

In This Edition · Three Pillars
I

Safety & Robustness of Verification

Robust verifiers must prevent reward hacking, resist harness specification gaming, and maintain alignment — scaling from outcome checks to process-level signals such as traces, plans, and tool-call sequences.

II

Environment-Grounded Verification & Simulators

For open-ended tasks lacking closed-form metrics, the environment itself is the ground truth. Faithful simulators of agentic tasks are emerging as a verification infrastructure layer.

III

Diverse & Heterogeneous Verifiable Signals

Beyond scalar rewards, holistic evaluation integrates user experience, cost and latency profiles, calibration, and multimodality — composed into reliable verification metrics.
